Most of my rejected talks didn't fail on the idea. They failed in the abstract.

So, I stopped guessing. I pulled all 203 sessions KCDC accepted in 2026 and had an AI agent take them apart. Title lengths. Colons. First lines and last lines. Which tracks were crowded, and which were wide open. The patterns were almost boring ... half the titles share one shape, and a third of the abstracts end by telling you exactly what you'll walk away with. (A few weak habits got accepted anyway. I'll show those too.)

In my opinion, speaking is one of the fastest ways to grow your career, and the abstract is the gate most of us never learned to write for. In this session, we'll look at what accepted talks have in common, why people with great ideas still get rejected, and how to get honest feedback on an abstract before a committee ever reads it. Then I'll run the whole process live on a real talk idea.

Whether you've never submitted a talk or you're collecting rejection emails, you'll leave with a structure for your next abstract, a review checklist, and more confidence to hit submit.

No more guessing.
